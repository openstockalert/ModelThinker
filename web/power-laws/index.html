Ch. 6 · Distributions

Rich get richer

When success feeds success, a few winners take almost everything.
Watch a fair-looking process produce a wildly unfair world.

Start with a small network. Add nodes one at a time. Each new node picks a friend to link to — but the probability it picks any given existing node is proportional to how many friends that node already has. Popular nodes attract more links, becoming more popular, attracting more links. "The rich get richer" (Barabási & Albert, 1999).

Watch node #1. The first few nodes get lucky purely by being early. They then keep winning. This isn't skill — it's compounding. Same dynamic that turns a slightly-better song into a chart-topping hit if listeners can see download counts (MusicLab, Salganik & Watts).

The signature: a straight line on log-log

Draw the histogram of node degrees. Most nodes have a handful of links; a few have hundreds. Zoom out on a log-log plot and the distribution is a straight line — the fingerprint of a power law. The slope tells you how heavy the tail is.

How unequal is it, really?

A Lorenz curve answers the question in one picture. On the x-axis: nodes ranked from least to most connected. On the y-axis: cumulative share of all links. A perfectly equal world would be a straight diagonal. Reality — anything preferential-attachment produces — bulges downward.

Where power laws appear. City sizes, earthquake magnitudes, casualties in wars, book and music sales, website in-links, word frequencies (Zipf), wealth, firm sizes, forest-fire areas. Different generating mechanisms, same shape — meaning fitting a straight line to log-log doesn't identify the cause.
