Where does the bell curve come from?

Take any process that adds up many small independent effects. The total is a bell curve.

The √n rule

The bell doesn't just appear — it also tightens. Doubling n only shrinks its width by √2. That's why quadrupling the sample size halves the standard error, not doubling.

Empirical std vs sample size · log-log

Slope on log-log should be −½ if the √n rule holds. Every step to the right = 4× the sample size = ½× the error.

The small-schools puzzle. A famous US education initiative funded splitting big schools into small ones because small schools kept showing up among the top test performers. But small schools also showed up among the bottom performers — for exactly the same statistical reason. σ/√n is bigger when n is small. Wider extremes on both sides. The signal wasn't school quality; it was sample size.
