One number decides an outbreak

Three compartments — Susceptible, Infectious, Recovered — plus two rates and a bit of arithmetic.
The whole shape of an epidemic follows from a single number: R₀.

Everyone starts susceptible (blue). A tiny number are infectious (red). Once you've had it, you're recovered (green) and can't catch it again. Watch what happens as we turn one knob.

Try R₀ = 0.9. The outbreak fizzles — the epidemic can't get off the ground. Now try R₀ = 1.1. Different world entirely. That's the single most important threshold in epidemiology.

Herd immunity, in one picture

Once enough of the population is immune (recovered or vaccinated), each infectious person meets fewer than one susceptible on average. The chain breaks. The magic threshold is 1 − 1/R₀:

  • Flu (R₀ ≈ 1.3): about 23% need immunity
  • COVID-19 ancestral (R₀ ≈ 2.5): about 60%
  • Smallpox (R₀ ≈ 5): about 80%
  • Measles (R₀ ≈ 15): about 93% — which is why WHO targets 95% coverage

Overshoot. Even after we cross the threshold, infections keep going for a while — the epidemic has momentum. That's why "just let it burn to herd immunity" over-counts what nature would deliver and under-counts the deaths along the way.

Time series & the peak

The classic textbook picture: S falls, I rises to a peak, R climbs to whatever the final attack rate is. Interventions can flatten the curve — lower the peak, buy time for hospitals — without necessarily changing the total infected.
